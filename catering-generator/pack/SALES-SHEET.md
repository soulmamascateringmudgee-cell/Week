## The problem

You've got a function for 80 on Saturday. You know the menu. Now you need to
know how much brisket to order, how much of everything else, what packaging,
and what has to happen on which day between now and then.

So you do it in your head, or on the back of the run sheet, the way you always
have. Sometimes you over-order and wear it. Sometimes you under-order and it's
a much worse day.

Same problem in a venue, just weekly instead of once: what should I be prepping,
what do I order Friday, and why do we throw out that much salad.

## What this is

A pack that teaches Claude how to do that maths properly, on your menu, in
about a minute.

You type in the guest count, the menu, the date and the service style. It gives
you back:

- **The order list, split by supplier** — meat, produce, dairy, dry goods, packaging, drinks
- **Raw weights, not served weights** — brisket loses half its weight; the list already accounts for that
- **The packaging and disposables kit** — including the chafing fuel, ice and gas that don't make the food order because they aren't food
- **A countdown with real dates** — what has to happen at T-14, T-7, T-3, T-1 and on the day
- **Three risks specific to that job**, with the fix for each
- **What it still needs to know**, as direct questions

For restaurants, cafes, kiosks and canteens there's a second mode: forecast
covers in, par levels out, plus a daily prep list, the delivery-day order, and
flags on anything that won't survive to the next delivery.

## What it isn't

It won't place your orders. It won't know your supplier prices unless you tell
it. It won't know your regulars.

And it's a planning guide, not a guarantee — you check the numbers against your
own records before you order, same as any prep sheet. First few jobs, run it
alongside how you do it now and compare.
